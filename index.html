Market risk / Basel MAR40 and EU CRR

Four risk classes.
One scaled requirement.

A transparent implementation of the FRTB simplified standardised approach.

Follow positions in interest rates, equities, commodities and foreign exchange through netting, weighting and offsetting to one capital requirement, under the Basel rules or the EU CRR. An open-source Excel/VBA prototype writes every intermediate value to a sheet.

01 / The calculation

Four charges, scaled and summed.

The simplified standardised approach keeps one charge per risk class and multiplies each by a scaling factor (MAR40.2). Risk-weighted assets are 12.5 times the requirement (MAR40.1). The Regime parameter selects Basel MAR40 or the CRR, and the headline of Results says which one produced the figures.

K = 1.30 IR + 3.50 EQ + 1.90 CO + 1.20 FX

Interest rate · × 1.30

Specific and general risk

Specific risk on the net position per issue, at the rate of its category, rating grade and residual maturity.

General market risk by the maturity method: one ladder of 15 time bands per currency, a 10% vertical disallowance, and horizontal disallowances within and between three zones.

Equity · × 3.50

Per stock, per market

Positions in the same stock or index net within a market.

Specific risk is 8% of the net position per single name, with a further 2% per index contract. General market risk is 8% of the net position per market.

Commodity · × 1.90

Simplified approach

15% of the net position per commodity, plus 3% of the gross position.

Positions are valued at spot prices and converted to the reporting currency.

FX and gold · × 1.20

Shorthand method

8% of the overall net open position.

That is the larger of the net long and the net short currency positions, plus the net gold position. Positions in the reporting currency are excluded.

Non-delta option charges are calculated outside the workbook, entered per risk class and added before scaling. Not implemented: the duration method, the commodity maturity ladder, the delta-plus and scenario methods for options and securitisation specific risk. The CRR regime multiplies the requirement by 0.9 up to 2029-12-31 (Article 495s(2)), charges the offset between zones 1 and 3 at 150% (Article 339), charges no index (Article 344), applies the FX de minimis of Article 351 and reports the size conditions of Articles 325a and 94; its other rules are those of MAR40. Every value of the CRR regime is illustrative: it is not validated, and nothing has been run in Excel for it. The README sheet of the template ↗ lists every limitation.

02 / Inside the workbook

Every step is written to a sheet.

  1. 01 / INPUT

    Enter parameters and positions

    The reporting date, the regime, the FX rates and one row per position, on four position sheets. The MAR40 constants are parameters too: a changed constant is flagged as a what-if run.

  2. 02 / CALCULATE

    Run the engine

    RunSSA reads the sheets, calculates in memory and writes values, not formulas: one row per input row with every intermediate, the maturity ladder and the offsets per currency.

  3. 03 / CHECK

    Read the findings

    An invalid row is rejected with its reason and listed; it is never corrected. The rules report errors and warnings, and a test catalogue compares outputs with the published example of MAR40.61 and with expected values for the sample data.

03 / Get started

Build from reviewable source.

1. Clone the repository

The workbook is not stored as a macro-enabled file. The repository holds its exported VBA source and a macro-free template with synthetic sample data.

git clone https://github.com/danielep71/K-FRTB-SSA.git cd K-FRTB-SSA

2. Build the workbook

The build needs Python 3.11 or later and no Excel. It writes a macro-enabled workbook under test-results/build/, stamped with its commit.

python tools/devbuild/build_workbook.py --configuration production

Opening and running that workbook in Excel is the project's next step; no such run is recorded yet.

04 / Evidence and boundaries

Know what has been checked.

Evidence available

Every change passes static checks of the exported VBA source, a reproducible build from one commit and the repository's own tests, in a pull request with a recorded review. The numerical cases hold three published values; the other 41 are my own derivations, and every value of the CRR regime is among them. I have run the prototype in LibreOffice's VBA mode only. None of this is an Excel run or regulatory validation, or a statement of compliance with the CRR.
